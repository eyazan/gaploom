## 2. Positioning [A]

* **Do not compete head-on with block/screw/sort hybrid-casual.** Those are UA-driven businesses with
  large ad budgets; a solo developer with a USD 0–100 budget cannot buy users profitably.
* **Target the "premium-feeling cozy puzzle" segment** (Monument Valley, A Little to the Left, Paper Trail):
  players who pay once, value art and novelty, and share satisfying clips.
* **Primary acquisition channel: organic short-form video** (5–15 s clips of a gap flying and a world
  rewiring). This is the only channel compatible with the budget.
* **Secondary: Steam + Web demo** for wishlists and puzzle-community press (Thinky Games, puzzle curators).

## 4. Key market risks [A]

1. **Discoverability**: the most likely failure mode. Mitigation: make the mechanic legible in 3 seconds of video (title screen animation already demonstrates it).
2. **Laser-puzzle look-alike**: light family resembles a saturated niche. Mitigation: always show gap transfer, use the motion family and the restoration dioramas in marketing.
3. **"Negative space" crowding** (Box or Void, Boxes Are Void!, jam games): avoid that phrase as the headline.
4. **Depth risk** (see `originality_audit.md`): P0 puzzles are short; premium buyers expect 3–6 hours of content.

## 5. What would change this analysis

* Real playtest metrics (tutorial completion, D1 return, purchase intent) from ≥30 testers.
* Wishlist conversion of a Steam page and view-through rates of 10–20 test clips.
